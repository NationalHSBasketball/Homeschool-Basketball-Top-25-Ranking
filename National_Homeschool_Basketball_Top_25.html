2026–27 National Homeschool Basketball Top 25

Ranking the nation's top boys varsity homeschool basketball players as the 2026–27 season gets underway.

Independent National Ranking 2026–27 Season Updated November 2026

The 2026–27 homeschool basketball season is underway, and the national landscape is already beginning to take shape. This ranking evaluates the nation's top players using previous individual honors, current role, team success, level of competition, individual ability and early-season performance. The order is based on who has the strongest overall case right now, rather than simply repeating last season's rankings.

The Top 25
NO. 25

Hunter Webber

THESA Riders

Hunter Webber earns the final spot in the Top 25 after making an early impact for THESA. The interior player gives the Riders a physical presence inside and has already shown an ability to produce against quality homeschool competition. His ranking is also supported by THESA's national résumé, with the Riders finishing No. 7 nationally last season. Webber has an opportunity to move up the rankings if he continues producing against stronger competition throughout the season.

NO. 24

Dallan Poll

CHAAMP Hawks

Dallan Poll earns No. 24 as one of the players representing CHAAMP in this year's national conversation. His ranking reflects his ability to contribute within a competitive Texas homeschool program and his potential to make a bigger impact as the season progresses. The players in this portion of the ranking are separated by relatively small margins, making Poll one of the players who could move quickly with a strong stretch of games.

NO. 23

Grady Coleman

CHAAMP Hawks

Grady Coleman comes in at No. 23 after establishing himself as one of CHAAMP's players to watch. His value comes from his ability to contribute within a balanced Hawks roster while competing against the strong homeschool programs throughout Texas. Coleman has the opportunity to strengthen his case considerably as more results become available.

NO. 22

Connor Quinn

CHAAMP Hawks

Connor Quinn takes No. 22 as another CHAAMP player with a legitimate national case. Quinn's ranking reflects his role within the Hawks and his ability to contribute against competitive homeschool opponents. A strong season against higher-ranked teams would give him a chance to move into the upper half of the rankings.

NO. 21

Drew Beery

KASH Eagles

Drew Beery has one of the stronger résumés in the bottom portion of the rankings after receiving 2025–26 Boys All-American Honorable Mention recognition. That honor establishes that he has already been recognized among the better players in homeschool basketball. KASH also finished No. 19 nationally last season, giving Beery experience within a nationally ranked program. Those two factors give him a clear case for the Top 25.

NO. 20

Grayson Hembree

KASH Eagles

Grayson Hembree comes in at No. 20 after also earning 2025–26 Boys All-American Honorable Mention recognition. Like Beery, Hembree played for a KASH team that finished inside the national Top 20 last season. His individual recognition and experience against national-level homeschool competition give him enough résumé to land in the Top 20.

NO. 19

Brayden VanAuken

NWA Hornets

Brayden VanAuken earns No. 19 as one of the leading players from the NWA Hornets. NWA finished No. 29 nationally last season, giving VanAuken experience playing within a nationally recognized program. His ranking is based on his individual ability and the expectation that he can be one of NWA's primary players as the season develops.

NO. 18

Dean Call

Aggieland Panthers

Dean Call is one of several Aggieland players to make the national Top 25. The senior guard brings valuable experience to a Panthers roster that already features several nationally ranked players. Aggieland finished No. 8 nationally last season, and Call's veteran presence gives the Panthers another experienced piece as they attempt to remain among the country's best programs.

NO. 17

Zach Kimber

Aggieland Panthers

Zach Kimber comes in at No. 17 as another experienced guard on one of the deepest rosters in homeschool basketball. Kimber plays alongside several players ranked higher on this list, giving him daily competition against high-level talent. His experience and ability to contribute within Aggieland's loaded rotation make him worthy of a spot in the Top 20.

NO. 16

Tommy Manhire

Aggieland Panthers

Tommy Manhire brings senior experience to an Aggieland team that finished eighth nationally last season. The 6-foot-1 guard provides another veteran option in a rotation filled with younger talent. His experience and ability to contribute within a nationally competitive program give him a stronger résumé than most players outside the Top 15.

NO. 15

Jared Demoff

HEA Firebirds

Jared Demoff earns No. 15 while representing one of the strongest programs in the country. HEA finished No. 2 nationally last season, making the Firebirds one of the toughest teams in homeschool basketball. Demoff's role within that level of program carries significant weight in this ranking. Playing against elite homeschool competition on a regular basis gives him a clear advantage over many players in the middle of the list.

NO. 14

Zach Walden

Aggieland Panthers

Zach Walden is one of the younger players on an Aggieland roster loaded with national-level talent. At 6 feet, the sophomore provides another perimeter option for a team with significant size and versatility around him. His development is important to Aggieland's long-term ceiling, and his ability to compete every day within one of the nation's deepest rotations gives him a strong case for the Top 15.

NO. 13

Zane Call

Aggieland Panthers

Zane Call ranks No. 13 because of his combination of size, versatility and potential. At 6-foot-4 as a sophomore, Call gives Aggieland another player capable of creating difficult matchups. His youth keeps him from being ranked even higher, but his physical tools and place within an elite homeschool program make him one of the more intriguing players in the country.

NO. 12

Devin Smith

DasCHE Spartans

Devin Smith is one of the biggest eye-test risers on the list. His performances against Texas homeschool competition have shown that he can take over stretches of games and create problems for strong opponents. His performance against both THESA and PT has particularly stood out, making him one of the more difficult players to leave outside the Top 15. If that level of production continues against stronger competition, Smith could quickly move higher.

NO. 11

Caleb Cox

Aggieland Panthers

Caleb Cox is one of the most important backcourt pieces on Aggieland's roster. The 6-foot junior point guard has the responsibility of organizing an offense that features multiple nationally ranked players. His ability to handle the ball, create opportunities and get Aggieland's talented roster into its offense gives him value beyond his individual scoring. That responsibility is a major reason he sits just outside the Top 10.

NO. 10

Timothy Krajca

Aggieland Panthers

Timothy Krajca cracks the Top 10 because of a rare combination of size and perimeter versatility. At 6-foot-6 as a sophomore, Krajca presents a difficult matchup while playing alongside several other nationally ranked players. His size gives him an immediate advantage, while his ability to play in a versatile role makes him more than just a traditional big. His upside is one of the biggest reasons he lands this high.

NO. 9

Xavier Carter

Dallas HSAA

Xavier Carter has one of the strongest résumés in the Top 10. Dallas HSAA finished No. 5 nationally last season and reached the national championship game, while Carter earned 2025–26 Boys All-American honors. That combination gives Carter both individual recognition and experience performing on one of the biggest stages in homeschool basketball. His championship-level experience makes him one of the safest choices in the Top 10.

NO. 8

Emmett Dyson

THESA Riders

Emmett Dyson is ranked No. 8 despite missing the season with a torn ACL because there is already strong evidence that he belongs in the national Top 10. THESA finished No. 7 nationally last season, and the Riders have struggled significantly more this season without Dyson running the offense. His absence has exposed how much the team relied on his ball-handling, playmaking and ability to create offense from the point guard position.

The strongest evidence for Dyson's individual ranking comes from his last season playing homeschool basketball. In eighth grade, he earned all-district, all-region, all-state and All-American honors, establishing himself as one of the top players in his age group. He was also a major contributor on both ends of the floor rather than being a player who depended on one specific skill. With that level of individual recognition, his role as a lead guard, and the clear difference in THESA's performance without him, No. 8 is a ranking based on proven ability rather than potential alone.

MHEA finished No. 9 nationally last season, putting Abram in an environment where he regularly competed against some of the best homeschool teams in the country. His individual All-American recognition combined with MHEA's national résumé makes him one of the most proven players in this year's field.

NO. 2

PT Warriors

That means McGarrah has been competing within one of the country's best homeschool programs while bringing a significant physical advantage to the frontcourt. NO. 1

Vince Walden

Aggieland Panthers

Vince Walden takes the No. 1 spot because he has the strongest overall combination of individual résumé, size, experience and team success. The 6-foot-4 junior was an All-District and All-Region selection last season and helped Aggieland finish No. 8 nationally.

What separates Walden from the other players at the top is that his résumé is strong across the board. He has already earned individual recognition, has experience competing at the national level, and returns to a talented Aggieland roster that gives him the opportunity to continue producing against elite homeschool competition. That combination makes Walden the strongest overall choice for No. 1 in the 2026–27 national homeschool player rankings.

The National Picture

The 2026–27 season has a strong mix of established All-Americans, nationally ranked-program veterans and younger players with significant upside. The top of the list is led by players who have already proven themselves against high-level homeschool competition, while several players in the middle and bottom of the rankings have the opportunity to make major jumps as the season continues. With more head-to-head matchups and postseason competition still ahead, this ranking will continue to evolve throughout the 2026–27 season.
